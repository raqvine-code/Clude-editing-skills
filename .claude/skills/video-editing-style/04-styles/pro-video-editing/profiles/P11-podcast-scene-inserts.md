# P11 Podcast Scene Inserts (guest clip, every key line becomes a designed scene)

Ref: 9735270fd1bb (34.5 s, 720x1280, 30 fps, -14.3 LUFS, LRA 2.7, 20 shots = 34.9 cuts/min) (V). English podcast guest clip (creator/entrepreneur advice). Studied 2026-11-20 with learnkit: 3 fps strips of the whole video, 12 stills, 3 animation strips, OCR, face and stereo-audio stats.
Use for: podcast or interview clips, founder/creator advice, any talking clip where the lines are quotable. Study only: never reuse the guest's footage or face.

## The after-edit look
- Bright A-roll: guest in a green tee, white curtains, podcast mic in frame, close framing (face width 35% of frame width, face centre y 0.31). A second WIDE camera angle is used once (2.2 s) as a breather.
- **Palette is taken from the guest's clothes**: deep green, cream, lime, plus crimson and mustard for contrast and black for the hard lines. Every insert reuses it.
- **Two-size light captions on the chest**: a tiny lead-in ("We posted that", about 2% of frame height) over one big keyword ("first video", box 5.9% of frame height, about 113 px at 1920). Light grotesque, lowercase, very tight tracking, white or a pale tint of the palette (cream-lime, olive). Centre x, y 50-60%. The keyword may sit BEHIND the microphone for depth. Words build additively with the voice; text lands on the word (+0.04 s). (M)
- **Coverage**: 60% of spoken words are on screen; some text is visible in 90% of frames. (M)
- **Name lower third**, thin outlined pill, bottom-left, first 2 s only.

## The core move: scene inserts
Every quotable line cuts to a full-screen designed scene, and the words are placed INSIDE the scene, each with its own type treatment:
| Time | Line | Scene and type |
|---|---|---|
| 2.07-4.53 | "$120,000 in pre-sales in three days" | Person reading a newspaper at a desk; white heavy number types digit by digit in 0.6 s (1 character per 2 frames) with a one-frame blur bump; cream heavy italic words land on the desk surface in perspective, one per spoken word |
| 7.67-9.97 | "you need to be prepared" | Retro office from behind; the words appear ON the old monitor's screen with a small sub-line |
| 9.97-12.13 | "what are the odds?" | Wide camera angle; dark-green words scattered left and right of the guest |
| 12.13-13.70 | "sometimes you will strike oil" | Grey 3D forecourt of a fuel station; giant dark-green extruded words stack downward BEHIND the building |
| 14.67-17.10 | "you learn what you don't even know" | Charcoal background; three curved ribbons (crimson, mustard, lime) sweep through with the words on their path, about 0.6 s each |
| 21.63-25.23 | "my goal is to get you to produce something that I literally can't do" | Three quick scenes: black with small white+gold caps (0.67 s) → notepad on a green cutting mat with sticker-outlined words building line by line (1.2 s) → black with white caps and the negative in red (1.73 s) |
| 27.53-29.57 | "you're asking it to expose" | Dark red room, gold-framed portrait with a smeared face, lamp; words stack over the face, then switch to red letter tiles; app icons slide up; 4 jump cuts of 0.3-0.7 s with a small tilt each |

Rules read from it:
1. A-roll and inserts alternate every 1-4.8 s; inserts take 41% of the runtime; A-roll never runs more than 4.8 s.
2. Inserts enter by hard cut, start a touch out of focus and snap sharp within 2-3 frames, then drift in slowly.
3. Text is diegetic: on a desk, a screen, paper, a wall, or behind an object. Never just pasted flat.
4. One type treatment per insert, all tied together by the palette.
5. Negative words are red; nothing else is.
6. The clip opens on a number result and ends on the payoff word ("entrepreneur") with no CTA.

## Numbers (M)
- Shot length median 1.46 s (p10 0.36, p90 3.27, max 4.83); 8 of 20 shots under 1 s.
- Speech 226 words/min, zero gaps of 0.5 s+ (airtight).
- Sound: wide stereo music up front (stereo layer only about 8 dB under the mid channel); 71% of stereo bursts land on a cut, so scene changes carry a hit or whoosh; captions do not.

## What to borrow
- Pick the 5-7 most quotable lines, give each a scene, and leave the rest as light two-size captions.
- Take the palette from the speaker's clothes or room.
- Put the big word behind a foreground object (mic, hand, building).
- Use a second angle or a wide crop once as a visual rest.

## Do not copy
Real brand logos (the reference shows a fuel-brand sign and AI-app icons): use generic marks unless the script names the brand. The guest's identity and the exact scenes.
