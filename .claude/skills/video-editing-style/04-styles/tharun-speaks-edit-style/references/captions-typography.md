# Captions and typography

Two type voices carry the whole style:

- **Caption voice**: a tight, bold, lowercase-friendly sans. It speaks every word.
- **Display voice**: a compressed, heavy, all-caps sans. It shouts the few words that matter and labels the graphics.

They never swap jobs. Captions are never all caps. Display type is never sentence case.

All sizes are for a 1080x1920 frame. Labels: Measured, Approx, Suggested.

### Colour and edge

| Property | On footage | On a white card |
|---|---|---|

| Outline | none | none |
| Background box | none | none |
| Shadow | soft black, falls slightly down and right | none |

Shadow recipe (Approx): two layers. A tight one, 5 px down, 12 px blur, 80% black. A wide one, 10 px down, 34 px blur, 50% black. The shadow is why white text stays readable over a brown shirt and bright hands without a box.

Always centred horizontally at x 540. Captions sit on the chest, under the chin. They never sit in the bottom 15% of the frame, where the app's own buttons and the desk props live.

Choose the y value once per video. Check three frames: the tightest punch-in, the widest shot and one graphic card. Keep the value for the whole video unless an object forces a single move.

### Beats

A beat is one caption state. It appears on the frame its first word is spoken and stays until the next beat replaces it.

| Rule | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Words per beat | 1 most of the time; 2 for a quick glue pair; 3 or more only in stacks | Measured |
| Median length | 6 frames (0.24 s) | Measured |
| Typical range | 4 to 10 frames | Measured |
| Fastest | 2 frames, for words like "is", "to", "a" | Measured |
| Entry | hard pop at final size | Measured |
| Exit | replaced by the next beat | Measured |

Glue pairs seen in the reel: a pronoun or article plus the next short word, such as "he built", "an App", "it was", "So if", "are the", "in the", "art of", "a link", "in bio", "just a", "one of", "out there". The same small words also stand alone when the speaker gives them time. Rule of thumb: pair them when the glue word lasts under 0.18 s and the pair is 10 characters or fewer.

Keep punctuation: commas and full stops stay on the word. Keep numbers as digits. Keep names and acronyms as their own beat.

### Emphasis stacks

A stack is the one caption form that holds still. It turns the key phrase of a sentence into a small composition.

```
you're                    <- lead-in: 0.72x, left edge of the keyword
seriously                 <- keyword: 1.30x to 1.75x
       missing out.       <- trailer: 0.72x, right edge of the keyword
```

| Part | Size | Alignment | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead-in | 0.70-0.80x of regular (about 84 px) | left edge of the keyword | Measured |
| Keyword | 1.75x for up to 5 letters, 1.45x for 6-8, 1.30x for 9 or more | centred in the frame | Measured |
| Trailer | 0.70-0.80x | right edge of the keyword | Measured |
| Maximum keyword width | 765 px, 71% of the frame | Measured |
| Line spacing | tight; lines nearly touch | Measured |

Measured examples of keyword scale: a 5-letter word at 1.73x, a 3-digit number at 1.73x, an 8-letter word at 1.43x, a 7-letter word at 1.31x, a 9-letter word at 1.31x.

How a stack builds:

1. The lead-in pops in small, already in its final place, on its word.
2. The keyword pops under it on its word. The lead-in does not move.
3. The trailer pops on its word.
4. The stack holds for 12 to 33 frames after the last part, then is replaced or cut away.

Variants:

- **Two lines**: lead-in over keyword. The most common.
- **Three lines**: lead-in, keyword, trailer on the diagonal.
- **Number build**: a big number first, then a small unit to its right on the same baseline, then a small line under the number.
- **Side word**: a short closing word such as "it." set small to the right of the keyword, on its baseline.
- **Card stack**: on white cards, a small first line and a bigger second line, both black, both centred.

Where stacks go:

- on the last phrase of a sentence or clause;
- right after a punch-in or right before a cut;
- on numbers, names, outcomes and the product being named;
- about once every 4 to 6 seconds. More than that and nothing stands out.

### When there are no captions

- While a giant behind-subject headline is on screen. The headline is the caption.
- During kinetic scenes with their own type.
- On the single black frame of a negative flash.

## 2. Display voice

### Font

The reel's display face is a compressed, heavy grotesque in all caps. It is narrower than every free condensed font tested.

| Use | Free match | Setting | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labels, kinetic titles, paper titles | Bebas Neue | width 80%, tracking -2% | Approx. Slightly lighter than the reel |
| Giant hook numerals and words | Anton | width 83% | Approx. Slightly heavier than the reel |

Measured proportions to match with any other font: one line's width is 4.76 times its cap height for a 12-character line, and the vertical stems are 14.5% of the cap height.

### The headline pattern

Headlines use the same three-part shape as caption stacks, at a bigger scale:

```
the story of this         <- small tag, caption font, sentence case
18 YO                     <- giant display caps or numerals
              student     <- small trailer, caption font
```

The small parts are in the caption font. Only the big part is in the display font.

### Behind the subject

Headlines over A-roll sit behind the speaker: the head and shoulders cover part of the letters. Three things make this read as depth, not as an error:

1. the text is big enough that it is readable with a third of it hidden;
2. a slight blur, about 2 to 4 px, and a soft white glow match the out-of-focus background;
3. the key characters are placed either side of the head, not behind the face.

Labels, arrows, icon badges and captions sit in front of the footage. Cards and logo badges sit behind the subject.

## 3. Quick checks

- Read the caption track with the sound off. If one beat needs two looks, it has too many words.
- Squint at a stack. Only the keyword should be readable.
- Pause on a headline frame. Can the headline be read with the head in the way?
- Count display-font words on screen at once. More than four is too many.
