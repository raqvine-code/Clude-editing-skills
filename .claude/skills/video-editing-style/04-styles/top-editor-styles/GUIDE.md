# Top editor styles

A playbook of five proven editing styles used by top creators and their editors. Use it when the editor asks to edit, plan, storyboard, caption or review a video "like MrBeast", "Hormozi style", "documentary style like Johnny Harris", "premium like Iman Gadzhi", or asks which style fits a video.

The job is to apply a style's rules to the editor's own footage and script. Never reuse a reference creator's footage, face, script, logo or branding.

For vertical talking-head reels in the Tharun Speaks style, use the `tharun-speaks-edit-style` skill instead. That skill is measured frame by frame and is more precise than anything here.

## How much to trust these rules

Read every rule with its label:

- **Published**: reported in public breakdowns of the creator's videos. Reliable as direction, not as an exact number.
- **Suggested**: standard practice for the genre that fits the style.

Nothing here was measured frame by frame. If the editor needs exact sizes, timings and colours for one creator, ask for two or three reference videos and measure them, then propose a dedicated skill.

## Rules every style shares

1. **Story first, effects second.** Fast cuts with no reason behind them do not hold viewers. Every cut, graphic and sound must have a job. Published.
2. **The hook makes a promise and starts paying it at once.** No intro, no logo, no greeting. Published.
3. **Something new every 10 to 15 seconds** in long-form: new information, a new location, a raised stake. Published.
4. **Every second earns its place.** Cut it if it adds no story, emotion or laugh. Never pad for length. Published.
5. **Match the pace to the audience.** Over-editing (endless zooms, whooshes, stacked jump cuts) tires viewers, older audiences most. Published.
6. **Sound is half the edit.** Clean voice first, then music that follows the emotion, then effects mixed under the voice. Suggested.

## Pick the style

| Video | Style |
|---|---|
| Challenge, stunt, competition, vlog with stakes | MrBeast challenge |
| One person talking to camera, business or advice, vertical short | Hormozi talking-head |
| Explainer, history, news, investigation, voice-over led | Johnny Harris documentary |
| Personal brand, luxury, agency or course creator, long-form | Iman Gadzhi premium |
| Study, productivity, tutorial, calm teaching | Calm educational |

If the editor names no style, pick from this table, say which one in one line, and carry on.

## Style 1: MrBeast challenge

Built for retention on long-form entertainment.

- **Hook:** the first 30 seconds decide the video. State the challenge, the prize and the stakes at once, and show the most extreme moment. Published.
- **Pace:** a visual change roughly every 1 to 4 seconds in high-energy stretches: cut, angle, zoom, graphic or text. Published.
- **Current era:** since 2024 the style is slower. More story, breathing room between scenes, fewer loud effects, longer emotional moments near the end. Use the slower version by default; use the frantic version only for short montages. Published.
- **Scale:** wide establishing shots to show how big the set, crowd or prize is. Published.
- **Tension:** always an open question: who is out next, how much time or money is left. Put a counter, timer or progress bar on screen whenever one exists. Published.
- **Text:** 3 to 4 words at most, bold and simple, plus arrows and circles that point at what matters. Published.
- **Structure:** hook, rules, first escalation, a new twist every 2 to 3 minutes, the hardest moment, the payoff, a short end. No recap. Suggested.
- **Sound:** an effect on each on-screen text and elimination, music that changes with every phase, silence before a result. Suggested.

## Style 2: Hormozi talking-head

Built for vertical shorts from one camera.

- **Open:** a bold statement or a contrarian take in the first frame. Captions on from frame one. Published.
- **Cuts:** every 1 to 3 seconds. Remove all pauses and filler words. Published.
- **Framing:** alternate between a medium shot and a close-up of the same take to fake a second camera. Published.
- **Captions:** all caps, heavy sans font (Montserrat Black or The Bold Font), word by word, with the spoken word highlighted in yellow or green. Published.
- **Colour:** dark, high-contrast grade. Accent palette of white, yellow, green and purple. Published.
- **Support visuals:** emojis, small image pop-ups, B-roll and simple UI animations that repeat the idea being spoken. Published.
- **Sound:** a pop or whoosh on each pop-up and zoom, low music bed under the voice. Suggested.
- **Warning:** this look is widely copied. Tell the editor that, and offer one change (font, highlight colour or caption position) to make it their own. Published.

## Style 3: Johnny Harris documentary

Built for voice-over led explainers.

- **Visual rule:** every animation, overlay and transition serves the story. Storyboard the visuals before editing. Published.
- **Maps:** animated maps with camera moves, highlighted borders and routes. Published.
- **Texture:** paper and film textures, film burns, light leaks, noise and grain over graphics and photos. Published.
- **Text:** typewriter-style type that types on, plus highlighted lines on real documents and headlines. Published.
- **Layers:** archive photos cut out and stacked with depth, moved slowly with eased keyframes. Published.
- **Structure:** open with a strange question or object, promise the answer, then alternate presenter-to-camera with archive and map sequences. Suggested.
- **Pace:** slower than challenge content. Hold a shot as long as it is still giving information. Suggested.
- **Sound:** cinematic score that builds, quiet room tone under the presenter, soft paper and click effects on graphics. Suggested.

## Style 4: Iman Gadzhi premium

Built to look expensive.

- **Look:** minimal, high-end animation with one consistent colour palette across every graphic. Published.
- **Graphics:** four kinds: on-screen text and icons, UI animations, AI-generated scenes, and 3D scenes. Published.
- **Captions on shorts:** lowercase, clean sans font (Montserrat), each word shifting from light to bold as it is spoken instead of changing colour. Published.
- **Texture:** vintage overlays and well-chosen stock footage, graded to match. Published.
- **Camera:** smooth zooms and moves made with adjustment layers, not hard punch-ins. Published.
- **Pace:** calm confidence. Fewer, cleaner effects. One idea on screen at a time. Suggested.
- **Sound:** carefully chosen music carries the mood; subtle effects only. Published.

## Style 5: Calm educational

Built for trust and long watch time.

- **Cuts:** fewer, with natural pauses kept. Suggested.
- **Graphics:** clean, light, with one accent colour and simple hand-drawn or handwritten notes that label the idea. Published.
- **Music:** lo-fi or ambient, barely audible. Published.
- **Option:** no burned-in captions and no music at all, for an intimate one-to-one feel. Published.
- **Structure:** state what the viewer will learn, number the points, show each point's title on screen, end with one clear next step. Suggested.

## How to apply a style to new footage

Work in this order. Give numbered, plain-language steps for the editor's own tool. Avoid jargon.

1. **Get the material.** Ask for the footage, or a script or transcript, plus platform and target length.
2. **Pick the style** from the table and say which.
3. **Find the story:** hook, stakes, escalation, payoff. Flag any missing part.
4. **Cut to the voice.** Remove dead air and repeats, to the level the style allows.
5. **Plan the visual layer** the style calls for: counters, captions, maps, 3D, notes.
6. **Plan the sound:** voice clean-up, music changes, effects.
7. **Check against the shared rules** and the QC list.

### What to hand back

When asked to plan an edit, return:

- an **edit plan table**: time in, length, what is on screen, cut or effect, text, sound;
- a **graphics list**: asset, the word or moment it lands on, how it moves;
- **build steps** for the editor's tool, in simple language;
- the **three weakest moments** and how to fix them.

When asked to review an edit, check it against the chosen style and the QC list, and report misses with timestamps.

## QC list

- Does the first 3 seconds make a clear promise?
- Any stretch with nothing new for more than 15 seconds?
- Any cut, zoom or sound effect with no reason behind it?
- More than one style mixed in the same video?
- More than one colour palette?
- On-screen text longer than 4 words (challenge and talking-head styles)?
- Music louder than the voice anywhere?
- Does the video end right after the payoff?
