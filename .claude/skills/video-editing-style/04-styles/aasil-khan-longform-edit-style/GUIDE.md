# Aasil Khan - long-form edit style

Reverse-engineered from ONE reference video: "I Made 8.85 Lakh From Content in One Month. Copy Me", first 707 s, 1920x1080, 29.97 fps (studied 2026-11-21: every shot viewed, 18 frame-by-frame strips, framing, colour and stereo audio measured). Full data: `references/video1-8-85-lakh.md`. Every graphic with its timing: `references/graphics-catalogue.md`.

Use this skill to apply the style to the editor's OWN footage, script and data. Never reuse the reference creator's face, footage, screenshots, numbers or channel assets.

## Evidence labels
- **Measured**: read from frames, audio or cut detection. Trust.
- **Observed**: seen in frames and strips, no number.
- **Suggested**: standard practice that fits the style; not confirmed in the video.

Limits: one video, and only its first 707 s (the ending was not supplied). Fonts are closest free matches. Treat rules as "this video's choices" until a second video confirms them.

## When to choose this style
| Choose this (Aasil Khan) | Choose `tharun-speaks-longform-edit-style` instead |
|---|---|
| The script is built on numbers, stages, a framework, screen proof ("how I made X", roadmap, tool or business lesson) | The script is a numbered list of life lessons and stories |
| Clean product-design graphics: white scenes, navy UI scenes, blue accent, no grain | Dark monochrome scenes with grain, giant behind-head headlines |
| Graphics teach: 42% of the runtime is full-screen graphics | Speaker carries it: 75% A-roll |
| Calm master (-16.5 LUFS), music mostly under the setup | Loud master (-10.5 LUFS), music in blocks |

## A. The three worlds (Measured shares)
1. **A-roll, 58%.** Low-key room, speaker in a dark tee, waist-up and centred (face 15.5% of frame width, eyes on the upper third), blue light strip on one wall, small warm practicals, heavy vignette. Very dark grade: median luma 27/255, cool violet-blue shadows, warm skin.
2. **White "keynote" scenes, 26%.** Off-white with a faint grid, black and blue headings, gradient blocks, star bullets. For data, lists and the roadmap.
3. **Navy "product UI" scenes, 12%.** Black with two royal-blue corner glows, four tiny corner dots like artboard handles, white semibold type, glass cards. For concepts, single words, big numbers, and as the frame around screen recordings.
4. B-roll, memes and montage: 5%.
The worlds meet by hard cut. A-roll and graphics alternate about every 4-5 s (speaker runs: median 4.7 s; away runs: median 3.8 s, 90% under 8.4 s, longest 21.5 s).

## B. Rules
1. **Cut rhythm.** 19 cuts/min; median shot 2.6 s, mean 3.1 s, p90 5.7 s. Mix: 14% under 1 s, 44% 1-3 s, 42% 3 s+. Steadier than Tharun (fewer sub-second shots). (Measured)
2. **The edit slows as the content deepens.** Setup and "why": 21-25 cuts/min. Detailed teaching: 10-16 cuts/min, with typed slides of 7-14 s and plain takes of 12-13 s. Sell fast, teach slow. (Measured)
3. **Mild punch-ins, not on every cut.** A jump cut re-frames by 1.21x (middle half 1.15-1.42x). Of consecutive A-roll cuts 35% punch in, 27% pull back, 37% keep the size. Takes of 2 s+ drift in about 0.8%/s. (Measured)
4. **Light-leak flash is the signature transition.** About 2 per minute (26 in 11:47), three quarters warm (orange → cream → violet tint), one quarter cool white. Around 10 frames: 2-3 frames of glow, one near-white frame with the cut under it, 6-8 frames of tint clearing on the new shot. Front-loaded: 20 of 26 in the first 5 minutes. Use it on an A-roll jump cut that changes topic, into or out of a montage, and when an overlay leaves. Never two within 2 s (this also keeps `my-editing-rules` "no flash trains"). Everything else is a hard cut, plus a two-colour block wipe (0.7 s) used twice. (Measured)
5. **Proof before promise.** The first words are a real number with its source on screen in the first 3 s, then the bigger total, then the gap in what people believe, then the agenda. (Measured: see section C)
6. **One recap chart for the whole video.** A single object that stands for the total, cut into slices per item. It opens the video and returns after each item is taught, adding that item's pin (% bubble, name, amount). 5 visits, 3-8.5 s each. Design a new object for each video; never copy the reference's. (Measured)
7. **A navigator for the framework.** When the script says "there are N parts", build one N-part shape with a matching list; all lit for half a second, then only the active part lit. Cut back to it between parts and once more as a recap. (Measured)
8. **A roadmap staircase that fills up.** One block per stage, each with its own colour, stepping up to the right on white. As each sub-topic is taught its name is added inside the stage block, with the details typed to the right. Earlier stages stay half visible at the left edge. This one slide carried 16% of the runtime in 21 visits. (Measured)
9. **Speaker + panel split.** For a concept that needs the face AND a definition: speaker on the left 52%, thin white line, navy panel on the right 48% with tag chip, one big word, one sentence, one micro-chart. Hard cut in. 1-5 s. (Measured)
10. **Everything builds with the voice.** Text types word by word at speaking pace (newest words coloured, then settling to the body colour; the key phrase keeps its colour). Cards, pins and pills pop one per spoken item, 0.12-0.3 s apart. Big words rise from a mask in about 0.5 s. Lines draw in 0.3-0.5 s. Text lands on its word: on median 0.03 s before the word starts, middle half from 0.23 s early to 0.21 s late. (Measured)
11. **Show how many points are coming.** Empty bullet dots fly in and line up first; each fills in when its turn comes. (Measured)
12. **Colour discipline.** One accent family (blues: #3367FE on navy, #3B8BFB in captions, #049FF9-#0275FA on white). One colour per stage (blue, purple, teal) reused on everything that belongs to that stage, including overlays on the A-roll. Red only for a danger word or a brand name in its own colour. Amber only for "currently selected". (Measured)
13. **Design-tool motif.** Corner handles around key text, selection boxes behind key words, a visible cursor that clicks, corner dots on every navy scene. It makes the video feel "built in a design app". (Observed)
14. **Screen proof is framed, not pasted.** Dark pages sit in a rounded window on the navy world; white pages run full-bleed like the white scenes; key phrases get a highlight box; scroll steps are cut short (0.3-0.8 s each). (Observed)
15. **Small graphics live around the speaker.** Photo tiles, chat notifications, thumbnails, pills and a tiny dot-tag pop around the head on the spoken word before the edit cuts away to a full-screen scene. (Observed)
16. **Text beside the head needs the speaker moved.** For a right-hand text block the shot is re-framed so the speaker sits at x 0.27-0.40 and the right side is darkened by a soft gradient. Used in 12% of the A-roll; the speaker is centred in 83%. (Measured)
17. **B-roll is self-shot and stylised.** The creator himself at a desk, with shutter-drag blur, ghost exposures and blurred frame edges, 1.6-2.6 s per clip, always with a small caption. Old videos of himself are used as "then" footage. No stock clips of strangers. (Observed)
18. **One humour beat.** A meme clip as a small card on black with an amber caption, 5 s, placed inside the serious "why" section. (Observed)
19. **Subscribe ask once, at about 10% of the runtime**, as a lower-third pill with a cursor click (3.1 s). It comes right after "I'm giving this away for free" and is backed by a stat shown on screen (the share of viewers who are not subscribed). (Measured)
20. **Calm, voice-led sound.** Master -16.5 LUFS, LRA 4 LU. Music clearly under the first minute (about 12 dB below the voice), easing down in minute 2, then faint or absent, with a lift when the last stage opens. Effects are soft and sparse: roughly 3 in 10 flashes and scene entries carry one; captions carry none. (Measured)

21. **Speech is cut airtight.** 212 words/min, median gap between words 0 s, only 11 gaps of 0.5 s+ in 11:47 and a single pause over 1 s, which is filled by the meme card. No dead air on A-roll. (Measured)
22. **One visual per named thing, in spoken order.** Four jobs named → four B-roll clips in that order; "seven other ways" → seven list rows; "a four-step process" → four empty bullets. Count the nouns in the line and build exactly that many beats. (Measured)
23. **Each item is taught in the same order**: what it is → what it earned and its share of the total (recap chart) → how to start (typed notes on the staircase) → one warning. The repeat makes the long video easy to follow. (Measured from the transcript)

## C. Setup recipe (0:00-1:19, Measured)
1. 0-3 s: one proof sentence with the number, chest caption, brand word in its brand colour.
2. 3-7 s: recap chart assembles (0.9 s), pins pop, crop-in, title types.
3. 7-10 s: the bigger total counts up beside the head.
4. 10-23 s: "most people think…" with photo tiles, a one-line navy word scene, a 3D list of what they are missing, the negative word in red.
5. 23-29 s: typed agenda, two bullets.
6. 29-49 s: who it is for (three pills), the promise ("you will get something valuable"), a preview of the questions on tilted cards, an honesty line ("this is not a get rich quick video").
7. 49-59 s: "this is the roadmap I wish someone showed me" (roadmap scene), "most people would put this behind a paid community".
8. 59-79 s: "I'm giving it for free", then the subscribe ask with its reason; framed recordings of the video page and of the analytics that prove the stat; lower third with click at 71-74 s.
The setup is long (the first teaching scene starts at 2:41), so it gets the fastest cutting and most of the flashes.

## D. Caption system (sizes Measured by OCR on 27 caption frames; text-box height as % of frame height, so multiply by 1080 for px)
- Selective, never full subtitles: only key lines get text. Across the whole video 39% of spoken words also appear on screen (captions, typed slide notes and scene text together), and some text is visible in 72% of frames (87% in the first minute). A-roll captions are dense in the first minute and rare later, when the staircase slide does the writing.
- Sentence case or lowercase. Regular-weight geometric sans, tight tracking, white with a soft shadow. No boxes, no all-caps (one red caps word appears on B-roll).
- **One coloured word per caption**: blue for the key word, red for the danger word, a brand's own colour for its name.
- Words fade in one at a time with the voice (2-3 frames each).
- Three positions:
  - (a) **Chest line**: one line, centred, y 72-82% (median 78%). Box 5-7% in the body (54-76 px at 1080), 9% for the opening sentence (96 px). Width 37-52% of the frame, up to 77% for the hook line.
  - (b) **Right-hand block**: x 51-94%, y 24-38%, speaker shifted left. Small lead-in line 6.4-8.9% over a key line 8.1-14.5% (1.3-1.6x bigger).
  - (c) **Left callout**: x 7-39%, y 27-40%. Small label 5.6% over a counting number 15% (162 px).
- Other sizes: one big word beside the head 8.5%; lists, tags and timeline labels beside the head 3.5-4.4% (38-48 px); B-roll captions 4.4% with a 8.9% red caps word; a centred statement on a white scene 14.4%.
- Decorations, one at a time: a hand-drawn underline that draws in 0.3 s; a pale selection box (#E3EDFE) behind the key words with the text turning blue; corner handles.
- On B-roll: small text at the top-left or centre; one variant with very wide word spacing.

## E. Workflow for new footage
1. Transcribe. Mark: the proof number, the total, the framework parts, the stages, each sub-topic, each number, each claim that needs screen proof.
2. Design this video's device set once: recap chart object, navigator shape, staircase with one colour per stage, tag chip, navy scene and white scene templates, caption styles.
3. Cut the A-roll; re-frame by 1.15-1.4x on about a third of the jump cuts; add a slow push on takes over 2 s.
4. Build the setup by section C, then cut hardest there (21-25 cuts/min, a flash every 10-20 s).
5. Per sub-topic: A-roll line with a caption → small graphics around the head → one full-screen scene (navy for the idea, white for the data, framed recording for the proof) → staircase visit that types the notes → recap chart visit when the item has a number.
6. Let the pace drop in detailed sections: hold the typed slide while the voice explains, 7-14 s is fine.
7. Sound: voice forward, music under the setup and at stage openings only, soft effects on about a third of flashes and scene entries. Reference master is -16.5 LUFS; for delivery use the house standard in `youtube-long-video-editing` (-14 LUFS, -1.5 dBTP) unless told otherwise.
8. Grade the A-roll low-key with cool shadows and warm skin; keep graphics clean (no grain).
9. Check against `mistakes-to-avoid`, render a 60 s sample, review, render.
