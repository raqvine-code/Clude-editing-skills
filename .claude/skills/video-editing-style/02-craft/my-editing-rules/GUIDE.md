1. Edit it (scratch in `pending videos/<project>/`).

## 7. Hooks and openings
- **Intro brief (Y2K, applies to every intro of a serious long video):** fast, premium, energetic, modern, never messy. MG highest priority: aggressive-but-clean kinetic type (rapid scale, position, tracking, rotation, masks, whips, reveals), layered animation instead of plain fades, micro-animations so frame is always alive, every graphic on the exact word/beat. Every animation earns its place (attention -> information -> emphasis -> emotion -> retention); viewer always knows where to look; subject unobstructed. Timing: cut dead space hard, land major words on the edit, anticipation -> impact -> release, three speeds (very fast hooks/emphasis, medium information, slow only impact/contrast). Black gradient masking where it helps readability. Y2K era as inspiration only (UI details, digital shapes, flashes, textures, retro-futuristic transitions), never noise. Audio and graphics one system (impacts on big animations, whooshes on fast moves, UI clicks on small graphics, risers on build-ups, controlled BGM under dialogue). Watch all, map hook/key statements/emphasis words/beats, plan every moment, execute consistently, no experimenting in the final. No template effects, excess zooms, glitches or repeated transitions. Final senior check: first-second attention, graphic speed, purpose of each animation, timing, motivated transitions, overload, premium feel, repeated effects.
- **Execution rules** (`video-intro-design`):
  - Don't re-time his voice to make an intro fast (Y2K take was 90 % speech, longest pause 0.355 s, only 0.758 s dead space in 33 s). Pace comes from camera (5-frame scale snaps, 3 s slow pushes), graphic density and transitions.
  - Verify script against his own transcript file, never ASR.
  - Every on-screen number comes from his script with a source line; nothing invented.
  - Phone first: sub-only impacts measured 20-26 dB quieter than transitions through a 300 Hz high-pass; every hit needs a mid knock + HF click (Claude's finding).
  - Delivery he scores himself: no BGM, master -17 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP, ship voice + SFX stems.
  - Cover bad frames with a motivated move, not a patch (eyes-closed second became a full-frame number card; a blink hidden by opening the shutter 0.1 s later).
- **Pro podcast-intro structure:** cold-open hero shots (colour, then steel-blue monochrome) -> two-tier hook card on its own colour gradient -> montage of artefacts behind keyed-out speaker -> credential card -> show-brand card, each card its own palette with one accent. See `motion-graphics-style` "Podcast / interview intro". Stills only so far: timing/easing/sound unlearned until he sends the clip.

## 8. Pacing and cuts
- MG rhythm: one meaningful visual change ~every 5 s, landing 2-3 s earlier than feels safe (replaces the old "every 7-8 s"). Each change carries something (a spoken word, number, source line, state change), never decoration. Inside comparison clips that must not be cut, add a light pointer beat. Check with numbers: log every entrance/cut/swap/counter/camera key; longest hold <= ~5 s.
- Keep his face on screen for human beats (Claude, unreviewed): evidence and explainers full-screen; reactions and emotional lines stay on his face (first pass had 71 % full-screen graphics; cut to 67 %).
- Three speeds, and the slow one must move: a `power3.inOut` push over ~3 s is motionless for its first 0.7 s and last 0.4 s; split long moves (`out3` off the cut, `in2` into the end), and turn any hold > ~1.2 s into a 3-4 % micro-drift.
- Snap OUT on a hero word (gives it room), snap IN on a negative word; a push-in on a reveal reads as panic.
- Speed contrast without touching the voice: camera snaps, 1-frame flashes, 4-frame match-cut cards, dither-through-black dissolves.

- **Audio redesign of a finished edit (2026-10-01):** senior editor + sound designer, preserve the picture. Analyse the whole video first; change only audio (remux untouched video); keep strong visual decisions. SFX intentional, modern, cinematic, frame-synced: no overused CapCut/meme sounds, no excess whooshes, no single SFX repeated; vary by action/emotion (whoosh, impact, riser, click, pop, UI, bass drop, transition, reverse, tension, ambience, foley); every major SFX has a reason; subtlety beats density. BGM follows sections (intro = curiosity, build-up = tension, fast = energetic rhythm, emotional = atmosphere, reveal/punchline = musical emphasis, ending = resolution), supports voice. Research references and adapt, never copy. QC: BGM fit, SFX timing, dialogue clear, unnecessary sounds, repetition, impact at key moments.
- Silence is not the tool; the hit is. Neither reference uses true silence while music plays (Tharun's only silence -> hit is a 90 ms gap before a stinger landing 10 ms before the cut). Impact comes from sub-bass hits and risers on the continuous bed.
- **SFX timing (measured):** talking-head cuts snap to the spoken word start (median 40 ms = one frame, 98 % inside a word), never the music beat. Tharun ~1 structural SFX per 10 s (riser 1.25-1.8 s into a hard stop/drop, one boom on hook cut, whoosh starting ~90 ms before a cut), no sound under captions or cards. Somrat (MG builds): sub impact +25 ms after each picture change, or impact/riser 0.27-0.5 s before a slow build, ~5 high-confidence hits per 10 s falling to ~2 by the end, ticks -10..-20 dB under card builds. Master -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP (Somrat); Tharun's +2.2 dBTP is clipped, don't copy.
- Reference creators for pace, SFX/BGM timing, silence, typography placement, grade: Tharun Speaks and Somrat Dutta (fast flow, flash edits, precise SFX/BGM, clean background removal/text behind subject, minimalist typography).
- SaaS motion-graphics videos (pure MG, no talking head) are a high-demand format he wants mastered; SFX carries the impact, every move gets a matched sound with exact timing (refs 09-11).

## 11. Transitions and effects
- **Three beginner-to-pro rules (training video, 2026-11-22):** make every icon / shape / card 25 % smaller, keep special effects for the one element that must grab attention, use dull colours (no pure black, white or bright red) - they are what separates amateur from professional animation - full numbers and QC in `beginner-to-pro-edit-tips`.
- **Every video gets text HIGHLIGHTING and a MATCH CUT:** a yellow brush-stroke wipe under the key line of any on-screen article/screenshot, and a keyword-anchored match cut (one highlighted word pinned, 5-7 sources flash-cut behind it every 3-4 frames). Mark `HL`/`MC` in every edit plan; at least one of each where the script allows. Recipe: `text-highlight-match-cut` (refs 12-13).
- **Motion-graphics floor for every short (user, 2026-11-26, after the client rejected video 25):** "use all skills... perfect skills: B-rolls, motion graphics and other things like a pro editor". Designed MG (list devices, contrast objects, HL / MC cards, UI cards, split / full scenes, objects behind the head, paper, plates) every 8 s, 3+ kinds, >= 25 % of runtime, plus literal B-roll per noun. Captions and word stacks don't count. Plan it in `PRE-EDIT.md` and check it with `01-process/before-edit/tools/pre_edit_gate.py` (CLAUDE.md Rule 0).
- Subtle black fades: short dip to black (~0.2 s down, 2 frames, 0.22 s up) into each chapter card and a short fade at the very end; not between ordinary cuts.
- **Never add zoom/position keyframes to his talking-head footage** (his CapCut exports already have them); if they leave a black band, cover with a designed element (header strip), don't re-frame. Exceptions: (a) split-panel slide: his footage may slide sideways ~540 px so he stays centred in the open space while a notes panel slides in (ref 58), no zooms; (b) a locked-off take with no keyframes (static background in every shot) may get subtle moves: clean punch-ins ~100 -> 110 % on jump cuts and hero words, slow 2-3 % pushes, never shaky/random; (c) client footage: keyframes allowed and expected (anticipate a word by ~2 frames; head >= 84 px below top edge); (d) shorts.
