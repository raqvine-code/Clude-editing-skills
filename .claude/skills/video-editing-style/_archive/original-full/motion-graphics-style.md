## Typography
- **Two-size hierarchy:** a small setup line (Montserrat Regular, ~25% size) plus one HUGE keyword (Montserrat ExtraBold/Black). Only the keyword must be readable.
- Build the sentence **word by word in sync with the VO**. New words arrive in **grey (#8a8a8a) and turn black (or white on dark)** when spoken.
- Stacked "staircase" lines: each line can be bigger and bolder than the one before.
- Word-replace beats: "The" → "Real" → "Question", one word at a time in the centre.
- Accents: a hand-drawn **ellipse circling** a key word or number, an animated **strike-through** on a negated word, and a handwritten-script final word (ref02 "perfect").
- Tech variant: **terminal window** (macOS dots, monospace, `>>` prompt, block cursor) that types, erases and retypes the line.
- **Luxury / serif variant *(ref05)*:** three tiers in one card. (1) Setup line in a high-contrast **italic serif** (≈6 % of frame width, black). (2) Keyword in **bold serif caps in the brand colour** (Rolex green), letter-spaced slightly, landing grey then solid. (3) A 2–3 line **proof paragraph** in a tight grotesk at ~2 % of frame height, centred, appearing last. Use for money, status, brand and career-value topics.
- **Single-word caption mode *(ref06)*:** a top layer that shows **one spoken word at a time**, white, plain geometric sans (Montserrat 500), ≈5–6 % of frame width, centred at ≈21 % from the top, **no shadow, no animation**. It runs on every word of the voice while a *separate* designed layer (big key lines, objects) carries the emotion. Use when the visual is the star and the sentence should not compete with it.
- **Wide-tracked typewriter CTA *(ref06)*:** "Save this post / your future self will thank you." light sans, letter-spacing ≈0.15 em, typed letter by letter, one small bookmark icon below. Good for save/comment/follow endings.
- **Distressed condensed display face *(ref06)*** for emotion words only (fear = red, greed = green); everything else stays clean sans. Never use it for more than two words per video.
- **Perspective kinetic line *(ref06)*:** a heavy condensed sans tilted in 3D, sliding out while shrinking ("no matter the trend.") as a punchline exit.

## Font-grading reel (keyframe pattern) *(YouTube Short "Top 5 Fonts I Use Daily as a Video Editor", youtube.com/shorts/9tEqrWm_xoA, 11.2 s — Deepak 2026-10-06: "this is one type of font grading, analyse the pattern and the keyframes")*
Read at 10 fps (every 0.1 s) plus full frames. **Seen** unless marked inferred.
**Structure:** two worlds, one hard switch. (A) 0–3.8 s **hook on a navy radial-gradient stage** with chrome/metallic type; (B) 3.8–11.2 s **white stage with one light-blue card** that holds each font sample. A white flash at 3.7 s is the switch.
**Hook keyframes (A):** "Editors" (0.1 s) fades up small and dim → "and" slams in 2× bigger beneath it (0.4 s) → "Content creators" slides in below (0.6 s); the three lines then **hold 1.0 s with a slow drift** (parallax, each line moves a few px at a different rate) → quick fade out (1.7 s) → "you" (1.8) / "should" (1.9) / "know these" (2.1) stack the same way → fade → a big serif-italic "5" blooms in (2.4–2.6 s, scale up with glow) → "fonts" lands under it (2.7 s) → hold with the glow pulsing → white flash. Every word: **arrive ~0.1–0.15 s apart, settle, hold, drift**; the type looks **brushed-steel/chrome** (gradient fill, dark edge, soft blue bloom) — inferred a metallic text preset.
**Card keyframes (B):** a fixed **light-blue rounded card** (≈ 60 % of the frame width, centred) with a **saturated-blue pill tab "5 best fonts"** on its top edge. Inside it a huge translucent **number (1…5) sits behind the font name** in a darker blue, like a watermark. Each sample is on screen ≈ **1.4–1.5 s**; the change is a **horizontal whip**: the old word exits left with motion blur while the new one enters from the right, both visible mid-frame for 2–3 frames (seen at 5.2–5.5, 6.8–7.1, 8.3–8.6, 9.9–10.2 s); the number swaps during the same whip. No fades, no scale pops inside the card — **one move, same direction, every time** = the rhythm.
**The five fonts (as set in themselves):** 1 **Grotesk** (= **Europa Grotesk SH Medium**, a clean Helvetica-style neo-grotesk, not rounded) · 2 **touche** (= **Touche Bold**, a geometric sans, not rounded) · 3 **tactic sans** (= Tactic Sans Bold Italic, wide extended italic tech face, two lines) · 4 **Achiko** (bold hand-drawn display with sharp uneven edges) · 5 **heading now** (= **Heading Now Medium Italic**, narrow grotesque). Exact names and weights come from the Short's description; profiles, licences and free stand-ins are in `fonts-and-colours` (researched 2026-10-09). Only 3 Tactic Sans weights are installed here.
**Grade:** A = navy #0B1E4A-ish → black radial, blue bloom; B = flat white, card #CFE0FF-ish, accent #2E5BFF-ish; the whole B section has no shadow except the card's soft drop.
**Reusable recipe:** hook on a dark gradient with 3-line stacked word slams (small → huge → small) + a glowing numeral; flash to a flat white stage; one fixed card + pill label; samples whip in from the right at a constant ~1.45 s cadence with the index numeral behind the word. Pair it with the "Cinematic font set" entry above for content.

## Layout & background
- Light scenes: warm paper **#E5E3DF**, faint dashed grid, black corner shapes (leaves or stars) half off-frame, logo top-centre, strong oval vignette.
- Dark scenes: charcoal **#1c1c1c**, white text. **Switch light ↔ dark** at mood changes (problem, doubt, hype) and switch back for the answer.
- Text sits in the top 40% and the object in the bottom 60% (or the object on one edge with text beside it).
- Breather beats: text only on an empty grid every ~10s.
- **Dotted-grid crosshair *(ref05)*:** thin dotted lines with round nodes forming a frame around the logo/keyword; dotted **circular guides** around a product; thin arc lines sweeping across the corners. It makes flat paper look "engineered".
- **Drifting paper *(ref05)*:** near-white paper with large soft cloud shadows moving slowly across it, so the background is never static; 3D gold/brand objects sit in the corners with cast shadows.
- **Dark stage *(ref06)*:** near-black frame, one glowing object centred, the caption above it, everything else empty. Colour arrives only through light (green/red spotlight from a top corner, glow under the object).
- **No-cut continuous scene *(ref06)*:** when the voice is one continuous thought, do not cut: let one object **morph** into the next (phone → figure → panel → crowd → stripes). Use hard cuts only at real topic changes.
- **Two text layers *(ref06)*:** literal single-word voice captions at the top + designed key lines in the middle. Never put both in the same position.

## Objects / visuals
- **One keyword = one object** (rocket = growth, magnifier = clarity, card = budget, hourglass = same problem, folders = posts/ads/campaigns, cash stack = charge).
- Mix of 3D renders, cut-out B&W photos, silhouettes and line-art, all desaturated to match.
- **UI mock-ups sell the idea:** YouTube-style progress bar + counters ticking up, macOS desktop with folders popping in, CRT monitor showing the text, receipt printing out, a phone that tears open.
- **Number counters** count up fast with motion blur.
- **Money counter in a phone frame *(ref06)*:** a balance that ticks down (≈−13 % every 0.25 s) to $0 while the chart light turns green → red; a tiny stick figure in the frame gives scale. Use for "lose money", "salary", "fees" beats.
- **Ghost figure + emotion orbs *(ref06)*:** a faceless grey figure (round head, half-moon shoulders) with five soft glowing orbs (purple, blue, green, white, red) above its head for "emotions / mindset / stress".
- **Glass chart panel *(ref06)*:** frosted rounded panel, a thin line drawing itself, a glowing spark riding the line tip; crowd silhouettes below it cheer (white) or panic (red, fewer heads).
- **Sphere through barrier *(ref06)*:** a glowing white sphere punching through tilted green stripes with light streaks = "break the pattern / plan".
- **Curves with pills *(ref06)*:** two sine curves (green/red) plus pill buttons that light up in turn and a marker sliding between them = "when X do A, when Y do B".
- **Meditating figure + orbit pills *(ref06)*:** glowing green figure with an orbit ring and small pills ("Mindset", "Market") = mindset/discipline.
- **Coin stack + rising curve *(ref06)*:** stack grows as the curve rises = compounding/long-run value.
- **Falling objects with motion blur *(ref05)*:** big banknotes (or any theme object) tumbling across the foreground with depth of field and heavy blur, passing *over* the type. Adds richness without hiding the keyword; keep them off the text centre for the last 0.5 s.
- **Product hero on paper *(ref05)*:** a cutout product enters from the frame edge with a slight rotation, a mint swoosh sweeps behind it, corner shadows of the brand mark sit under the type.
- Tech (ref02): top-down device on a desk with a **HUD blueprint** (ruler, year ticks, callout lines, "DEVICE OVERVIEW", "v16" labels). Swap the device's colour and model on every beat and rotate the whole blueprint between eras.

## Motion
- Objects enter **from the frame edges with motion blur** and settle on a soft ease-out (small overshoot). Can also scale up from a dot.
- Hold each visual **~2–3s**. Micro-changes every 0.25–0.5s inside a shot (a word, a colour swap, a counter tick).
- Constant slow push-in or drift, so nothing is ever fully still.

## Transitions
- Default is **blur-out → blur-in** (defocus).
- For energy: **directional whip with motion blur**, **radial zoom/spin blur**, or a **glitch/digital distortion** (tech).
- **Warm pink/red light-leak flash** only right before the end card.

## Colour grade
- Monochrome (saturation ≈ 0). Contrast carries the look, with true blacks on light scenes.
- Tech/dark variant: cool grey, **lifted blacks (~10%) and rolled-off whites (~88%)** for a matte film look.
- Always: oval vignette, fine film grain, a few dust specks.
- Allowed accent colour: the red of a progress bar or a brand colour, used once, small.
- **One brand colour + one metal *(ref05)*:** Rolex green for text, gold for objects, on near-white paper (luma ≈200, saturation ≈2). Nothing else.
- **Colour = emotion *(ref06)*:** tint the whole scene by mood: **green = up / calm / skill**, **red = down / panic / loss**, white-grey = neutral or the person. Switch the tint on the word that changes the mood (prices climb → green, prices drop → red). Near-black base (luma ≈26).

## Sound
- VO on top, a quiet low music bed under it (ducked), and an **SFX on every new word/object** (pop, click, soft whoosh), roughly every 0.4–0.8s.
- Typing clicks for terminal/typewriter text, a whoosh on whips and blurs, a glitch hit on glitch cuts, a riser into the end card.
- Last ~1.5s: the logo sting while the music fades out.
- **Bed by hand, not by ducker *(ref07 / ref08, measured)*:** the pros do not sidechain the music to the voice; the bed sits at a fixed level per section (−11…−13 dB under a hook, −17…−22 dB under dense explanation, −8 dB in a drop, relative to speech RMS) and is removed 0–1 times per video, never for silence's sake.
- **Two densities *(ref05 / ref06)*:** a luxury brand story is **sparse** (≈1 soft hit per beat, ≈0.8 per second, mean −24 dB): let objects land quietly. A dark explainer is **dense** (≈1.2 hits per second, mean −16.5 dB): a hit on every object change, counter tick, orb, slam and pill. Pick the density from the topic, then stay consistent for the whole video.

## Story shape
Myth/hook → "But…" → the real question → list or contrast (pills, "Sometimes it's X") → consequence → payoff line → logo sting (dark swirl background, logo glitch-writes on, then fade to black).
- **Brand story shape *(ref05, 11 s)*:** "how X became Y" (product hero) → proof ("luxury brands in the world" + paragraph) → contrast ("they were never just A, they were selling B") → what it really is (STATUS) → breather → studio credit card.
- **Explainer shape *(ref06, 32 s)*:** shock number (balance → $0) → cause ("they misunderstand one thing: emotions") → the rule of the world ("markets run on fear and greed") → the cycle (green up, red down) → break the pattern (planning) → the fix (curves + actions) → mindset → long-run payoff (coins) → punchline ("no matter the trend") → save CTA.

## Highlight + match cut *(ref12 Dhruv Rathee-style tutorial, ref13 match cut — 2026-09-30)*
- **Brush-stroke highlight on evidence screenshots:** rough-edged yellow marker, −1…−2° tilt, 1.3× line height, revealed by a left→right mask wipe (0.4–1.5 s) starting on the spoken word; one line per beat, same yellow all video. *(ref12)*
- **Keyword-anchored match cut:** the highlighted word stays pinned at one screen position while 5–7 paper-textured, motion-blurred source pages flash-cut behind it every 3–4 frames (17 cuts / 2.3 s), loop the set if longer, whoosh-in, hit on the last cut, white corner flash out. *(ref13)*

## Editorial print-grain explainer *(refs 14–40, Deepak's "refrences for claude" folder — 2026-09-30)*
Deepak's reference board for long-form explainers (analysis: `experiment/07_references_folder_editorial_explainer.md`). Use it as the **default look for 16:9 long-form explainers** on careers, companies, economics and history.
- **Paper world:** off-white grid paper (#F1F1EE, 80 px grid at 1080p, grid lines ~5 % ink), ink #393939 (never pure black), a white speckle grain screen-blended over all ink so shapes and type look risograph-printed, thin 2.5 px sketch outlines, faint vignette, a slow 1 % push so the page is never frozen.
- **Type:** tight bold titles centred at the top (Montserrat 800, −0.035 em), grey sub-labels, footnotes bottom-left ("*Illustrative", "Source · …") — any forecast, dataset estimate or illustrative number carries its qualifier on screen.
- **One icon family:** blue engineer-at-laptop (Indian teams) vs dark (foreign), client building with lit yellow windows, retro beige PCs for the 1990s, dot-people inside pyramid tiers, "1 icon = 10 people" grids.
- **Charts:** pastel blue pairs for money, pastel yellow for headcount, or speckled dark-grey bars; bracket annotations with a signed value (green up / red down); dashed connectors for "−80 %".
- **Continuity:** one diagram carries the story and returns in new states (pyramid → contract → AI-wired pyramid → pyramid with gaps). Morph, don't replace.
- **Dark premium mode** for AI beats: black stage, glowing periwinkle orb, thin white curved connectors, cream label cards with glossy round 3D icons.
- **Talking-head treatments:** framed rounded card on black with a YouTube progress bar and glowing gradient-dot bullets (agenda/recap); cream keyword card lower-left with a white label above and a big blue keyword (the everyday caption); light split panel with coloured-dot bullets (lists and advice while the face stays on screen).
- **Evidence:** designed quote/news cards with a yellow marker wipe on the key phrase (HL) and source line; never fake a screenshot.
- **Balance *(Claude's rule from the Y2K edit, not yet approved by Deepak)*:** keep full-screen graphics ≈ 55 % of a talking-head explainer and break any full-screen run longer than ~25–30 s with a face beat carrying a keyword card; move advice/opinion sections to the split layout so the creator stays on camera.

## Black faded overlay *(tutorial "How to Add a Black Faded Overlay Like Iman Gadzhi in Premiere Pro", Mujahid Fixes, youtu.be/NcxcQmM4x0I, 1:05 — 2026-10-01)*
Deepak asked to learn this technique and apply it "naturally integrated", keeping the original footage as untouched as possible (framing, colour, camera movement, subject). What the tutorial actually shows (stepped through frame by frame — it contains **no text system**, only the overlay; the Before/After title and a check-mark icon are its only graphics):
- **One rectangle on its own track above the footage** (Premiere: Rectangle tool → Essential Graphics shape, same duration as the clip). The footage is never graded, scaled or cropped.
- **Fill = Linear Gradient with BOTH stops pure black.** Top stop opacity **0 %**, bottom stop opacity **100 %**. While building it passes through white→black (looks grey); the finished state is a black-to-transparent fade, never grey.
- **Placement:** the rectangle is dragged over the lower part of the frame: the fade starts around mid-torso (~55–60 % down the frame), is clear of the face, and reaches full black at the bottom edge (measured on the final frame: luma falls from ~215 to 0 over the lower ~40 %).
- **Why it works:** it darkens only where text will sit, so white type stays readable on any wall or light, and the subject keeps their natural look above it.
- **CapCut equivalent (not tested in this session):** put a black clip/colour on an overlay track, add Mask → Linear, rotate it so the transparent side points up, set Feather high, and slide the mask line to ~55 % height; lower the overlay opacity to taste. The Premiere rectangle also works as a transparent PNG gradient on the overlay track.
**Deepak's before/after reference (podcast host, vertical, 2026-10-01), measured by eye on the frame:** the fade begins at the waist (~55–60 % down the frame), the face, hair, shoulders and mics are untouched, it is nearly black by ~80 % and **pure black over the last ~15–20 %** (lap and hands vanish). It is a smooth, band-free, true-black ramp, i.e. the tutorial's 0 % → 100 % gradient, not a grey veil. The footage above the fade is identical to "Before" (no grade, no zoom). So the strong version is the right one for this look; use the strong end (100 %) at the very bottom, and keep the text inside the black area.
**Usage rule (Deepak, 2026-10-01):** learn-only. Use sparingly, on main long videos, as a light fade behind selected highlighted text; never on every video, never as a blanket pass, and don't apply it to a finished edit unprompted. ## Y2K / retro-futurist, executed premium *(built on the Y2K intro, 2026-10-01)*
Full research: `experiment/claude6_intro/work/studies/y2k.md`. Build method: `video-intro-design`.
- **Pick the accent by measuring the plate, not by taste.** On Deepak's set, skin (Lab hue 52°), the door (52°),
  the lampshade (54°) and the warm wall (60°) all sit inside one 9° hue band — so amber/orange, the reflexive
  Y2K choice, dissolves into his face (ΔE 33–41 from the lamp). **Cyan `#00E5FF` at 215° is the maximum
  separation available** on this footage.
- **`#00E5FF` is 1.03:1 on his bare wall and `#C0C0C0` chrome is 1.22:1 — both are invisible unplated.** That is
  not a style note, it is a measurement, and it is what makes the black gradient self-policing: the accent
  physically cannot leave the `#06070A` plate. Windows-98 navy `#000080` is the opposite case — 10.8:1 directly
  on the wall, the most legible chromatic colour available with no gradient at all.
- **One hero chrome word per film**, with a *moving* specular (static chrome is a sticker, moving chrome is a
  material): an 11-stop vertical ramp with a hard horizon at the 50 %/52 % pair, plus a dark rim, no bevel.
- **Period UI appears in one frame and never eases** — a Win98 dialog that fades in destroys the illusion.
  Integer px, `image-rendering: pixelated`, `-webkit-font-smoothing: none`, authentic system colours
  (`#C0C0C0 #DFDFDF #808080 #0A0A0A`, caption `linear-gradient(90deg,#000080,#1084D0)`).
- **Ordered-dither (Bayer) through black** is the premium transition — it reads as craft, not as a filter. 4–6 px
  cells minimum at 1080p; random noise instead of an ordered matrix looks like compression.
- The whole glitch budget for a 33 s film is **one** degauss. Amber is permitted only inside a fully plated frame.
- Avoid: butterflies, 8-point sparkles, chrome on more than one word, holographic post filters, synthwave
  (that is 1984, not 1999), and anti-aliased "pixel" UI.

## Montage pacing from launch films and creator clips *(refs 50–56, the AI Tech News ep. 1 folder — 2026-10-02)*
Measured frame by frame in `experiment/09_references_50-56_ai_news_ep01_pacing.md` (sheets + `metrics/report.json` in `experiment/frames_ref50-56_ainews/`). Deepak: *"I want only pacing."*
- **The accelerando ladder *(ref55, Anthropic's Opus 5.5 film, 38 shots in 20 s)*:** three ~1 s establishing shots → eleven ~0.5 s → twenty-three 0.2–0.48 s (median 0.32 s = 8 frames) → one 4.1 s release plate with the wordmark. Ratio ≈ 1 : ½ : ⅓ : ⅕; the release is 20× the shortest shot.
- **An anchor licenses speed:** every plate shares one silhouette (a convex horizon curve) and the words sit at its apex in the same face and size — the eye never re-orients, so 5 cuts/s read as rhythm. Without a constant element that rate is noise.
- **Words at 1/5 the picture rate:** no text for the first 8.7 s; "There's" / "more to" / "discover" each arrive **on a cut, every 5th cut**, and hold through 5 / 5 / 13 shots.
- **Flip brightness on the cut:** 21 of 37 cuts reverse the luma direction (plates alternate dark / bright, 26 ↔ 230 at the peak). The luminance jolt is what makes a 5-frame shot register.
- **Sound follows the picture, not a grid:** the bed shows no tempo lock; cut-to-hit offsets are +15…+40 ms (one frame after the cut), 24/37 within ±35 ms; one silence right before the first cut; hits thin out once the words carry the rhythm.
- **Zero-cut scale reveal *(ref54, OpenAI's Sol & Luna teaser, 15 s)*:** no cuts — dust → galaxy → star → planet under a 0.272 s tick; type-on at ~24 letters/s, a new line every 1.05 s, the last line scaling up for 1.1 s into a hard out (end on the peak, not a settle).
- **Shape-to-letter title *(ref52, OpenAI's Astra film)*:** pastel geometric placeholders (circle, triangle, diamond, square, dot) appear at letter positions and become letters in a non-linear order (S · A · A · T · R): 1.67 s build, 2.5 s hold, pale-yellow extended grotesk (inferred), "INTRODUCING GPT-6" small above; 0.7 s bookend shots around a 4 s title hold.
- **Comparison-clip grammar *(refs 51, 53 — Blender villa, Canva portrait)*:** split screen, logo + label per panel, the same burned timecode in both (proof of equal time), one long hold (12.5 s / 16.5 s), the **only edit is a 0.27 s black-flicker reveal** (2–3-frame blinks on alternating panels), then a 4–4.5 s final hold with a credit line. Never cut inside a comparison.
- **Race punchline *(ref56, Berman's TypeSafe WikiRace)*:** 2.25 s anticipation on the empty panels and the button → click → 0.75 s → "Finished · 1st place" greys out the winner while the others keep moving for ≥ 2 s. The losers still moving *is* the joke.
- **Raw screen captures *(ref50, Krcha's Blender train)*:** the usable seconds are the mode switches (solid → wireframe at 8.5 s = the "3,295 objects" reveal) and the ~1 s selection clicks; orbit filler is cut.
- **Reference-folder rule:** his unnamed screenshots were posts (Krcha, Edgex/Karan, Charlie Hills, Dario, Altman, Musk, Coxon), launch pages (TypeSafe, Grok 4.7, Grok Bot), OpenAI benchmark charts and event key art — identify every file from its pixels and tie it to a script timestamp before planning the cut.
